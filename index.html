Breezeway SMED Workshop · machining and winding cell

The brief

Breezeway Fans Ltd (a fictional company) machines and winds the motor housings for its Desk and Tower fans in one cell, inside its domestic focused factory. One worker runs all six machines: they load and unload each machine (30 s per machine), walk 38 s per loop, and do every changeover. Today the cell runs daily batches: 45 Desk, then 30 Tower.

The operations manager wants smaller batches to cut stock and lead time, and is worried about a possible +15% order from a retailer. In this workshop you reduce the CNC lathe changeover with SMED, then work out its effect on capacity, batch size, lead time and cost.

Key facts

  • Demand 1,500 fans a month = 75 a day: 60% Desk, 40% Tower.
  • 2 shifts × 7.5 h = 54,000 s a day. Takt = 720 s.
  • Worker cycle time 218 s (6 × 30 s + 38 s walking).
  • Winder per fan incl. loading: Desk 510 s, Tower 630 s; weighted 558 s. The winder sets the pace.
  • Labour £18 per hour; cell running cost £95 per hour.

Task 2: can the cell afford the changeovers?

For each resource the changeovers load: N × S × (1 − SMED) + D × p ≤ T. N = model switches per day, S = changeover time per switch, D × p = production time per day, T = 54,000 s. The SMED % applies to every machine in the cell.

Work it out by hand on your worksheet first, then press Show the check.

Task 4: EOQ versus lean (Tower housings)

S = changeover time × labour cost per hour (£18). The cell running cost is left out because it is paid whether or not the cell changes over. H = £60 × 25% a year is an assumption: try your own values. The winder limits Tower output to 54,000 ÷ 630 = 85.7 a day while the cell runs Tower.
